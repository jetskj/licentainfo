# Barr et al. (2015) — The Oracle Problem in Software Testing: A Survey

**Key:** `barr2015` · **Type:** journal article (survey), *IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering* 41(5):507–525 · **DOI:** [10.1109/TSE.2014.2372785](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2014.2372785) · **PDF:** not available (closed access)

**Cluster:** Test oracles · **Used in:** Method §definitions; the argument that O0/O1/O2 are different oracles

## What it establishes

Given an input, deciding whether the observed behaviour is the desired one is the **test oracle problem**. The survey covers oracle automation by modelling, specifications, contract-driven development and metamorphic testing, and notes that when none of these is adequate, **the human remains the final source of oracle information**, bringing informal specifications and domain expectations.

It also frames every oracle — including the human — as a cost/benefit trade-off: reducing cost and increasing benefit.

- None; this is a conceptual survey.

## Why it matters for this thesis

This is the theoretical backbone of the thesis's central sentence: *every check you can run answers one question well and is blind to the other*.

Concretely:

- **O0** is a partial oracle over developer-written assertions. It says nothing about behaviour the suite does not assert.
- **O1** is an oracle over one specific attack path. It is precise about that path and blind to legitimate use.
- **O2** is an oracle over user-visible flows. It is blind to authorization semantics that still return 200.
- **Adjudication** is the human oracle — exactly what Barr et al. describe as the last resort. The thesis makes that last resort explicit, rubric-based, blinded and sampled for re-judging, instead of leaving it implicit.

The survey also justifies *not* trying to build a perfect oracle: the thesis measures the cost/benefit of each partial oracle rather than claiming completeness.

## Where to use it

- Method §1 (Definitions): define "oracle", "gate" and "stage" using this framing.
- Method §4: introduce each gate stage as a distinct oracle with a stated blindness.
- Threats to validity: the human adjudicator is an oracle too, with known costs; cite the agreement protocol from `zheng2023`.

## Limits

A general testing survey; it does not discuss patches, repair, or security. Do not attribute claims about patch correctness to it. It supports the *structure* of the argument, not the empirical rates.
