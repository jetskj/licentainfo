# Hu et al. (2025) — SoK: Automated Vulnerability Repair: Methods, Tools, and Assessments

**Key:** `hu2025` · **Type:** SoK paper (arXiv:2506.11697); accepted at USENIX Security 2025 · **DOI:** [10.48550/arXiv.2506.11697](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2506.11697) · **PDF:** `papers/hu2025_sok-avr.pdf`

**Cluster:** LLM vulnerability repair · **Used in:** Related work; gap statement; framing of "validation"

## What it establishes

A systematisation of automated vulnerability repair (AVR) organised around the **three steps of the AVR workflow**:

1. vulnerability analysis,
2. patch generation,
3. **patch validation**.

The authors assess AVR tools for C/C++ and Java. Because existing C/C++ tools are evaluated on different, often small datasets, they build **VUL4C**, a benchmark of **144 vulnerabilities with exploits and patches**, and use it to evaluate **seven AVR tools**; they also evaluate **two Java tools** on the third-party **VUL4J** dataset. The paper closes with future research directions.

## Numbers worth citing

- 144 vulnerabilities in VUL4C.
- Seven C/C++ AVR tools evaluated; two Java tools on VUL4J.
- Three-stage workflow with validation as a named stage.

## Why it matters for this thesis

This is the single most important framing citation. It establishes that **patch validation is a recognised stage of vulnerability repair**, not an implementation detail — and it shows that the community evaluates tools on heterogeneous, often small datasets.

What it does *not* do is quantify validation's error rate: the SoK systematises methods and benchmarks, but the thesis's question — how often does the acceptance decision say yes to a bad patch, and which check is responsible — remains open. That is the gap sentence, and `hu2025` is the strongest evidence that the gap is real rather than imagined.

It also gives the thesis a benchmark context for its optional extension: VUL4J (via `bui2022`) and VUL4C are the natural places to test whether the findings transfer beyond the synthetic app.

## Where to use it

- Related work, opening of the vulnerability-repair subsection: "the workflow has three stages; this thesis is about the third".
- Gap statement in the introduction and in the synthesis of related work.
- Future work: replication on VUL4C/VUL4J.

## Limits

A systematisation, not an empirical study of gate reliability. It reports tool performance on benchmarks, which depends on the benchmark's tests and oracles. Do not cite it for a false-accept rate.
