# Chen et al. (2019) — SequenceR: Sequence-to-Sequence Learning for End-to-End Program Repair

**Key:** `chen2019` · **Type:** preprint (arXiv:1901.01808); published in *IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering*, 2021 · **DOI:** [10.48550/arXiv.1901.01808](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1901.01808) · **PDF:** `papers/chen2019_sequencer.pdf`

**Cluster:** Learned program repair · **Used in:** Related work (pre-LLM learned repair); the "correct patch" measurement problem

## What it establishes

SequenceR is an end-to-end neural repair approach: a sequence-to-sequence model trained on source-code edits, using a copy mechanism to handle the unbounded vocabulary of code. It is trained on **35,578 samples** curated from open-source commits and evaluated on **4,711 independent real bug fixes** plus Defects4J.

Results reported in the abstract:

- it predicts the **fixed line perfectly for 950 / 4,711** testing samples;
- it finds **correct patches for 14 bugs** in Defects4J;
- it captures a wide range of repair operators without domain-specific top-down design.

## Numbers worth citing

- 35,578 training samples; 4,711 test bug fixes.
- 950/4,711 exact fixed-line predictions.
- 14 Defects4J bugs with correct patches.

## Why it matters for this thesis

SequenceR marks the transition from template/search-based repair to learned repair, and it illustrates the measurement problem the thesis inherits: the headline "correct patches" number is small relative to the test set, and it depends on how correctness is judged. It is a useful baseline sentence in related work: "learned repair improved fluency but did not remove the need for an acceptance oracle".

It also supports the thesis's decision not to use a repair-accuracy metric as its outcome. SequenceR optimises the generator; the thesis measures the judge.

## Where to use it

- Related work, paragraph on learned repair before LLMs.
- Introduction, one clause in the historical arc: GenProg → learned repair → LLM agents.
- Do not use it as a state-of-the-art comparison; it is a 2019 model on Java.

## Limits

Java, pre-LLM, general bugs (not security). The 950/4,711 figure is exact line prediction on a curated test set, not end-to-end repair success; do not conflate the two. The published TSE version may differ in details from the arXiv version used here, and Crossref's record for the TSE version is incomplete (early access), which is why the entry is keyed to the arXiv version.
