# Le, Alimadadi & Ko (2024) — A Study of Vulnerability Repair in JavaScript Programs with Large Language Models

**Key:** `le2024` · **Type:** conference paper (companion), WWW 2024, pp. 666–669 · **DOI:** [10.1145/3589335.3651463](https://doi.org/10.1145/3589335.3651463) · **PDF:** `papers/le2024_js-vuln-repair.pdf` (arXiv:2403.13193)

**Cluster:** LLM vulnerability repair · **Used in:** Related work; justification of the Node/Express stack

## What it establishes

JavaScript is the most widely used language in web development, and secure JavaScript is hard to write. The paper studies **ChatGPT and Bard** on **finding and fixing security vulnerabilities in JavaScript**, using real-world vulnerabilities, and specifically asks how the **context included in the prompt** affects whether the model produces a correct patch.

The abstract's conclusion: LLMs are promising for automatic repair of JavaScript, but **a correct fix often requires an appropriate amount of context in the prompt**.

## Numbers worth citing

- Two models: ChatGPT and Bard.
- No single headline rate in the abstract; the qualitative finding is about context sufficiency.

## Why it matters for this thesis

This is the closest published work to the thesis's stack (JavaScript, web application vulnerabilities) and it supports a specific methodological choice: **the context the repairer sees is a variable, not a detail**. The thesis restricts the agent to the vulnerable function plus one level of callers and must document that restriction in an appendix. Le et al. give empirical reason to treat that as consequential.

It also reinforces the gap: the paper evaluates whether the model finds and fixes the vulnerability, not whether an acceptance gate can tell a correct fix from a plausible one. A model that fixes "often, given the right context" is precisely a model whose output needs independent verification.

## Where to use it

- Related work, JavaScript/web subsection.
- Method §agent: justify the context-restriction rule and the decision to log exactly what the agent saw.
- Threats to validity: context sensitivity means results may not transfer to other context budgets.

## Limits

A short companion paper (5 pages); two 2023-era models; no gate, no adjudication, no per-stage error rates. Do not overstate its findings beyond "context matters for correctness in JavaScript repair".
