# Jain et al. (2025) — LiveCodeBench: Holistic and Contamination-Free Evaluation of Large Language Models for Code

**Key:** `jain2025` · **Type:** conference paper, ICLR 2025 · **DOI:** [10.48550/arXiv.2403.07974](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2403.07974) · **PDF:** `papers/jain2025_livecodebench.pdf` (ICLR proceedings; arXiv:2403.07974)

**Cluster:** Evaluation methodology · **Used in:** Related work; Threats to validity; the leakage argument

## What it establishes

Existing code benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP) are saturating and vulnerable to contamination. LiveCodeBench collects **new problems over time** from LeetCode, AtCoder and CodeForge, so that evaluation data post-dates model training. It also covers broader capabilities than code generation: **self-repair, code execution, and test-output prediction**.

At publication it hosted **over 500 problems published between May 2023 and May 2024**, and evaluated **18 base LLMs and 34 instruction-tuned LLMs**, reporting empirical findings on contamination and potential overfitting in existing benchmarks.

## Numbers worth citing

- 500+ problems, May 2023 – May 2024.
- 18 base + 34 instruction-tuned models evaluated.

## Why it matters for this thesis

Two distinct arguments:

1. **Contamination.** An earlier direction of the thesis concerned benchmark contamination: when a benchmark ships its answers (documented solutions or reference fixes), a reported success may measure lookup rather than repair. LiveCodeBench is the methodological statement of why a benchmark must be able to show that the model has not seen the answers. The thesis now uses a self-built app, which is the strongest form of contamination control: the model cannot have memorised it.
2. **Benchmark validity is measurable.** LiveCodeBench evaluates the evaluator (contamination, overfitting), which is the same move the thesis makes with O4: measure the gate, not only the patch.

Use it to justify reporting the construction date and the novelty of the apparatus, and to explain why results on public benchmarks would not settle the thesis's question.

## Where to use it

- Related work, evaluation validity.
- Method §apparatus: contamination argument for building a new application.
- Threats to validity: the app is new, but the *bug patterns* are standard and may be memorised in general form; acknowledge this.

## Limits

Competitive-programming problems, not security or repair. It does not measure acceptance gates. The contamination-free property is about problem novelty, not about the absence of similar code in training data.
