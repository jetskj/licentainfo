# Liu et al. (2021) — A Critical Review on the Evaluation of Automated Program Repair Systems

**Key:** `liu2021` · **Type:** journal article, *Journal of Systems and Software* 171:110817 · **DOI:** [10.1016/j.jss.2020.110817](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jss.2020.110817) · **PDF:** `papers/liu2021_evaluation-review.pdf` (University of Luxembourg repository)

**Cluster:** Patch correctness / evaluation · **Used in:** Related work; Threats to validity; metric design

## What it establishes

APR evaluation has shifted from counting **plausible** patches to counting **correct** patches, a shift the authors describe as necessary after the overfitting findings. But they argue the **latest evaluation metrics can still be biased**: design decisions — in both the approach and the evaluation setup — are not always fully disclosed, so the impact on repair performance is unknown and computed metrics are often misleading.

Their response is a critical review of how patch-generation systems are evaluated, plus **eight evaluation metrics** proposed for fair assessment. They demonstrate with experimental data on **11 baseline repair systems** that the proposed metrics expose caveats in the literature.

## Numbers worth citing

- Eight proposed evaluation metrics.
- 11 baseline APR systems used in the demonstration.

## Why it matters for this thesis

This is the methodological license for the thesis's reporting choices:

1. **Report false accepts and false rejections separately**, never a single "accuracy". The thesis's definitions section already argues that the two errors have opposite consequences; Liu et al. support the claim that aggregate metrics hide design-dependent bias.
2. **Disclose the evaluation setup.** The thesis must publish the prompt, the context restriction, the test suite, the adjudication rubric and the gate implementation. Otherwise the numbers are not interpretable — the exact failure Liu et al. describe.
3. **Do not treat "correct patch rate" as a neutral number.** It depends on the benchmark, the suite and the criterion for correctness, which is why the thesis uses a human rubric rather than a test-derived label.

## Where to use it

- Related work, end of the patch-correctness subsection: the evaluation critique.
- Method §7 (analysis): justify per-class error rates and Wilson intervals instead of a single score.
- Threats to validity: cite it when acknowledging that the benchmark is constructed by the author.

## Limits

The paper reviews APR evaluation, not security-patch acceptance, and not LLM agents. It does not give a rate for any particular gate. Cite it for the *principle* that metrics embed design choices, not for a specific numeric claim about LLM repair.
